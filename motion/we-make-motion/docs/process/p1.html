We Make Motion* — ProcessOverview

At a glance

The brief asked for a sleek, fluid, 30-second motion sequence featuring five words (We, Make, Motion, Without, Code) with percussive sound and distinct effects tied to key moments. The result is a short film that argues its headline for 26 seconds and then footnotes itself.

Everything was built in code: a single HTML page draws each frame on a canvas as a pure function of time, and the soundtrack is synthesised from oscillators and filtered noise by the same page. No video editor, no audio workstation, no samples, no stock assets. One cue sheet drives both picture and sound, which is how every bounce, slam and wipe lands on its hit.

We Make Motion* — ProcessThe brief

The brief, and the prompt I wrote myself

What was asked

“Self prompt and create a modernistic, sleek and fluid motion design sequence that really shows off your capabilities, not just in design but in the principles of motion and motion graphics … feature some key words: We, Make, Motion, Without, Code … 30 seconds, a percussive sound and distinctive sound effects that align with key moments.”

How I read it

Three things were being tested at once: visual design, the craft of animation (the twelve classic principles and the modern motion-graphics vocabulary), and audio-visual sync. A pretty title sequence would only cover the first. So the piece had to demonstrate principles on screen, legibly, and every one of them had to be timed to sound.

The five words also carry an irony: a film saying “without code” that is built entirely from code. I decided to use that rather than hide it. The film makes the claim with full conviction, then adds an asterisk.

Assumptions I made instead of asking

  • Format 16:9 at 1920×1080 and 60 fps, because fluid motion reads better at 60.
  • Output An MP4 master plus a live, scrubbable web version.
  • Sound Percussion-led, with tuned accents in C major pentatonic so plucks and pings sit together.

The self-prompt

// creative brief, written before any code
Make a 30-second film that says
"We make motion without code",
built entirely from code, and let it notice.

Rules
1. One protagonist: a flamingo dot. It opens
   the film, appears in almost every scene,
   and closes it.
2. One tempo: 120 BPM. Every cut, hit and
   transition lands on the 16th-note grid.
3. One cue sheet: picture and sound read the
   same timestamps, so sync can't drift.
4. Every frame is a pure function of time,
   so it can be scrubbed, rendered offline
   and motion-blurred without re-simulation.
5. Each word gets its own motion language:
   WE assembles, MAKE constructs, MOTION flows,
   without is still, CODE decodes and collapses.
6. Show the principles, don't list them.
7. Restraint: five colours, three typefaces,
   flamingo belongs to the dot.
8. End on the joke. Keep it dry.

The rules above shaped every later decision. Rule 4 is the technical one that everything else depends on.

We Make Motion* — ProcessConcept & structure

A claim in eight beats, and one footnote

TimeBeatMotion language
0:00The dropA dot falls and bounces. The film introduces its protagonist and its tempo.
0:02WEOne becomes many: mitosis, then a particle swarm that assembles the word.
0:06MAKEConstruction: a grid, measurements, letters drawn stroke by stroke.
0:10MOTIONFlow, then excess: a wave, a marquee, a build that collapses to a line.
0:16withoutAbsence: silence, stillness, a thin serif. The camera dives into the ink.
0:18CODEDecoded, struck through, dropped.
0:22The claimAll five words, each entering with its own verb.
0:26The fine printThe full stop becomes an asterisk. The footnote types itself.

The dot is in almost every scene: it drops and bounces, splits into the particles, lands in the counter of the A, surfs the wave, becomes the tittle of the i, returns as the full stop, turns into the asterisk, and finally blinks out where it started.

We Make Motion* — ProcessDesign system

Five colours, three typefaces, one dot

Palette

Ink #111116Dark ground (drop, WE, CODE, fine print) and type on light scenes. Slightly blue-black, never pure black.
Chalk #F1EFE9Light ground (MAKE, without) and type on dark and blue scenes. Warm off-white.
Ultramarine #2D2DF5The loud ground: MOTION and the claim. Electric, close to International Klein Blue.
Flamingo #FF5A87Reserved for the dot and its deeds: echo trails, the strike-through, the asterisk.
Butter #FFD34ECode only: brackets, selection highlights, the caret.

I avoided the common defaults (near-black with a single acid accent, cream with terracotta). Three grounds alternate scene to scene, giving the cut rhythm a colour rhythm too: ink → ink → chalk → ultramarine → chalk → ink → ultramarine → ink.

Typography

MOTION

Unbounded 900. Wide, geometric and heavy. The shouting voice: WE, MOTION, the claim.

without

Bodoni Moda Italic. A high-contrast Didone whisper. Used for one word only, so the contrast lands like a change of tone of voice.

<CODE/>

Martian Mono. A wide monospace that rhymes with Unbounded’s proportions. CODE, the source code backdrop, the HUD and the footnote.

MAKE uses none of them: its letters are a custom monoline alphabet built from 13 stroke segments with round caps and joins, so they can be drawn on like a pen.

A thin HUD slate frames most scenes: chapter label, tempo, running SMPTE timecode, bar number and a four-square beat indicator, so the viewer can literally see the beat.
